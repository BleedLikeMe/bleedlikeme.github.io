Welcome to the LSE Simulator

You start with £100,000 and trade on a simulated London Stock Exchange with 80 real UK-listed stocks across all major sectors. Your goal is to grow your portfolio through smart trading, risk management, and reading the market.

How a Day Works
Pre-MarketThe clock begins at 08:00. Prices are set from the previous close. Place orders in advance.
Market Open08:00–16:30. Prices tick continuously. News events, earnings, circuit breakers, and economic data fire throughout the day.
Market Close16:30. Day-end summary shows your P&L, challenge result, achievements, and leaderboard position.
New DayClick "Next Day" to continue. Prices carry over; dividends are paid; new events are generated.
Speed Controls

Use the 1× 2× 5× 10× buttons to accelerate game time. At 10×, a full trading day takes about 5 real minutes. Press Space or ⏸ to pause.

Tip: Run at 2× or 5× normally, then pause when you want to study a chart or place a complex order. Use 10× to fast-forward quiet periods.
Scoring & Leaderboard

Your score is your total portfolio value at the end of each day. The top 10 scores are saved locally. Complete daily challenges for bonus cash rewards.

Saving & Loading

The game auto-saves to localStorage at the end of each day and when you close the modal. It loads automatically on page refresh. Use the ↺ button to start a completely fresh game.

Buying & Selling Stocks
Opening the Trade Modal

Click any row in the market table to open the detail modal for that stock. You'll see the live price chart, key statistics, and trade controls.

Buying (Going Long)

Enter a quantity and click BUY. The cost is (price in pence ÷ 100) × quantity + £9.95 commission. Prices are quoted in pence (GBX) — a 1,234p stock costs £12.34 per share.

Tip: Use the "Max" link to automatically fill in the maximum quantity you can afford.
Selling

Switch to the SELL tab. You can sell any portion of your holding. Profit/loss is shown before you confirm. Commission of £9.95 applies.

Short Selling

The SHORT tab lets you borrow shares and sell them immediately, profiting if the price falls. To close a short, use the COVER button.

Short EntryYou receive cash for the shares sold short. A borrow cost accrues daily.
ProfitIf the price falls, covering costs less than your original sale — the difference is profit.
RiskIf the price rises, losses are theoretically unlimited. Use stop-loss orders to manage risk.
Commission

Every trade (buy, sell, short, cover) costs a flat £9.95 commission. This is deducted from your cash. High-frequency trading on cheap stocks will erode returns quickly.

IPOs & Rights Issues

IPOs appear as pop-up notifications. Subscribe for an allocation at the listing price — you may receive fewer shares than requested. Rights Issues appear as special limit orders — eligible shareholders are offered new shares at a discount to market price.

Order Types

All order types are set from the Limit/Orders tab in the trade modal. Active orders are shown in the sidebar Orders tab. They execute automatically when price conditions are met.

Order TypeDescriptionUse case
Limit BuyBuy when price falls to your targetEnter a position at a better price than current
Limit SellSell when price rises to your targetLock in profit at a specific target
Stop-LossSell if price falls below a levelAutomatically limit downside risk
Take-ProfitSell when price reaches a target (above current)Secure gains without watching the screen
Trailing StopSell when price drops X% from its highest point since order placedRide upward trends while protecting gains
Trailing Stop Example

You set a 5% trailing stop on a stock at 500p. The price rises to 600p — your stop automatically moves to 570p (600 × 0.95). If the price then drops to 570p, the order fills. If it rises further to 700p, the stop moves to 665p.

Price Alerts

Set alerts from the Alerts tab. When the price crosses your threshold, a banner notification fires. Unlike orders, alerts don't automatically trade — they just notify you.

Tip: Combine a trailing stop with a take-profit for a "let winners run but protect gains" strategy. Set take-profit at your stretch target and trailing stop at 5–8% to exit gracefully if momentum fades.
Reading the Charts
Candlestick Chart

Each candle represents one price period. Green candles mean the price rose that period (close above open). Red candles mean the price fell.

The body spans the open→close range. The wicks (thin lines) show the high and low. Long wicks indicate indecision or reversal pressure.

Volume Bars

Volume bars at the bottom of the chart show how many shares traded each period. High volume on a big price move confirms the move. High volume with little price change suggests buyers and sellers are balanced.

Moving Averages (MA20, MA50)
MA20 (blue)Average of the last 20 periods. Tracks short-term trend.
MA50 (orange)Average of the last 50 periods. Tracks medium-term trend.
Golden CrossMA20 crosses above MA50 — bullish signal.
Death CrossMA20 crosses below MA50 — bearish signal.
Bollinger Bands

Three lines: the MA20 in the middle, with upper and lower bands at 2 standard deviations above/below. When price touches the upper band, it may be overbought. When it touches the lower band, it may be oversold. Band squeeze (bands narrowing) often precedes a big move.

RSI (Relative Strength Index)

Oscillates 0–100. Above 70 = overbought (consider selling). Below 30 = oversold (consider buying). Divergence between RSI and price can signal reversals.

MACD

Shows the difference between the 12-period and 26-period EMAs (the MACD line) vs a 9-period signal line. The histogram shows the gap between them. When MACD crosses above the signal line, it's bullish. Histogram turning green after being red = early bullish signal.

Tip: Don't use indicators in isolation. Look for confluence: RSI oversold + price at lower Bollinger Band + positive volume = stronger buy signal than any single indicator alone.
Understanding the Numbers
Individual Stock Metrics
P/E RatioPrice ÷ annual earnings per share. A P/E of 15 means you pay £15 for every £1 of annual profit. Lower P/E = cheaper relative to earnings, but may signal problems.
Dividend YieldAnnual dividend ÷ share price × 100. A 5% yield on a 200p stock means 10p paid per share per year. Dividends are paid daily in this simulator.
BetaHow sensitive a stock is to overall market moves. Beta 1.5 means a 1% market rise tends to produce a 1.5% rise. Beta 0.5 = defensive stock, half the market's swings.
52W High/LowHighest and lowest prices in the past 52 weeks. Breaking above 52W high = strong bullish momentum. Holding at 52W low may be a value entry.
EPS / EarningsStocks have quarterly earnings events. A beat sends the price sharply up; a miss sends it down. Watch for earnings dates flagged on chart.
Portfolio Metrics
Portfolio BetaWeighted average beta of all holdings. Shows overall market exposure. >1.2 = aggressive; <0.8 = defensive.
Sharpe RatioReturn per unit of risk. >1 is good; >2 is excellent; <0 means you'd have done better in cash. Calculated from daily P&L history.
Realised P&LProfit or loss locked in by completed trades. Unrealised P&L is the current paper gain/loss on open positions.
Borrow CostDaily charge for holding short positions. Erodes profits on shorts held overnight — cover them if the trade isn't working.
Portfolio construction tip: Mix high-beta stocks (tech, aerospace) for growth with low-beta defensives (utilities, healthcare) for stability. A beta-neutral portfolio performs regardless of market direction.
